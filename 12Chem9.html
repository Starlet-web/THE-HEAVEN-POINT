Coordination Compounds

Complex Compounds in which the metal atoms are bound to a number of anions or neutral molecules by sharing of electrons by forming coordinate bonds are called coordination compounds.

Ex:- K3[Cr(C2O4)3], [CoCl2(en)2]Cl

1. Werner's Theory of Coordination Compounds

In coordination compounds metals shows two types of linkages (valences) - primary and secondary.

  • The primary valences are normally ionisable and are satisfied by negative ions.
  • The secondary valences are non-ionisable. These are satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions. The secondary valence is equal to the coordination number and is fixed for a metal.
  • The ions/groups bound by the secondary linkages to the metal have characteristic spatial arrangements corresponding to different coordination numbers.

Ex:- [Co(NH3)6]Cl3

  • Primary valences = 3 (satisfied by 3Cl-)
  • Secondary valences (coordination no.) = 6 (satisfied by 6NH3)

Octahedral, tetrahedral and square planer geometrical shapes are more common in coordination compounds of transition metals.

Thus, [Co(NH3)6]3+, [CoCl(NH3)5]2+ and [CoCl2(NH3)4]+ are Octahedral entities, while [Ni(CO)4] is tetrahedral and [PtCl4]2- is square planar.

3. Sidgwick's Concept of Effective Atomic No. (EAN)

The ligands donate a certain no. of e⁻ to the central atom metal ion. The total no. of e⁻ on the central atom including those gained from ligands through bonding is called the effective atomic no. of the central metal ion.

EAN Calculation Examples

Ques 1: EAN of Co in [Co(NH3)6]3+
Ans: Electron in Co atom = 27 e⁻
Electron in Co⁺³ = 27 - 3 = 24 e⁻
Electron donated by 6(NH₃) = 6 × 2 = 12
EAN = 24 + 12 = 36

Ques 2: EAN of Cr in [Cr(NH3)6]3+
Ans: e⁻ in Cr atom = 24 e⁻
Electron in Cr⁺³ = 24 - 3 = 21 e⁻
Electron donated by 6(NH₃) = 6 × 2 = 12
EAN = 21 + 12 = 33

4. Basic Terminology

CO-ORDINATION ENTITY:- A coordination entity constitutes a central metal atom or ion bounded to a fixed number of ions or molecules.

Ex:- Ni(CO)₄, [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺

CO-ORDINATION SPHERE :- The central metal atom/ion and ligands attached to it are enclosed in square brackets [ ] and is collectively termed as the coordination sphere. The ionisable groups written outside the bracket are called counter ions.

Ex:- K₄[Fe(CN)₆], [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ → [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is the coordination sphere. The counter ion is Cl⁻.

CENTRAL ATOM / ION :- The metal atom or ion to which ligands are directly bound. Acts as a Lewis acid (electron pair acceptor). Usually a transition metal due to variable oxidation states and availability of vacant d-orbitals.

Ex:- [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ → Co³⁺

OXIDATION NUMBER OF CENTRAL ATOM :- The oxidation number of the central atom in a complex is defined as the charge it would carry if all the ligands are removed along with the electron pairs that are shared with the central atom.

Ex:- [Co(CN)₆]³⁻ → x + (-6) = -3 ⇒ x = +3

LIGANDS: The ions or molecules bounded to the central atom/ion in the coordination entity via coordinate bonds are called ligands. These are Lewis base (electron donor). There may be simple ions such as Cl⁻, small molecules such as H₂O and NH₃, larger molecules such as H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ or even macromolecules such as proteins.

Types of Ligands :-

  • UNIDENTATE LIGANDS: When a ligand is bound to a metal ion through a single donor atom. Ex:- Cl⁻, H₂O, NH₃.
  • DIDENTATE LIGANDS: When a ligand can bind through two donor atoms. Ex:- C₂O₄²⁻ (oxalate), H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ (ethane-1,2-diamine) (en).
  • POLYDENTATE LIGAND: When several donor atoms are present in a single ligand. Ex:- Ethylene diamine tetraacetate ion (EDTA)⁴⁻.
  • CHELATE LIGAND: When a di- or polydentate ligand uses its two or more donor atoms to bind a single metal ion. Chelate complexes tend to be more stable than similar complexes containing unidentate ligands.

Denticity: The no. of ligating group is called the denticity of the ligand. Denticity refers to the no of atoms with which a ligand binds to a metal ion.

Ex:- NH₃ denticity 1, en denticity 2.

5. Ambidentate Ligands

Ligand which has more than one donor atoms but at a time can ligate through one atom is called ambidentate ligand.

For example:- NO₂⁻ and SCN⁻ ions. NO₂⁻ ion can coordinate either through nitrogen or through oxygen to a central metal atom/ion.

M ← NO₂ (Nitro-N)
M ← ONO (Nitrito-O)
M ← SCN (Thiocyanato-S)
M ← NCS (Isothiocyanato-N)

6. Nomenclature of Co-ordination Compound

  • Rule 1: The ligands gives first and the central atom name in last.
  • Rule 2: ORDER OF NAMING LIGANDS:- If the coordination sphere of given complex compound contain various type of ligands, they are named in alphabetical order. Prefixes Di, tri, etc. are not to be considered while determining the alphabetical order. Ex:- [Co(NH₃)₄NO₂Cl]⁺ → Tetraamminechloridonitrocobalt(III) ion
  • Rule 3: NAMING OF NEGATIVE LIGANDS:- If the names of anions ends in ide, ite, ate. It is replaced by O. Ex: SO₃²⁻ (Sulphito), SO₄²⁻ (Sulphato), CH₃COO⁻ (acetato).
  • Rule 4: NAMING OF NEUTRAL LIGANDS:- H₂O (Aqua), NH₃ (Ammine), CO (Carbonyl), NO (Nitrosyl), CS (thiocarbonyl), NS (thionitrosyl).
  • Rule 5: NAMING OF POSITIVE LIGAND:- NO⁺ (Nitrosonium ion).
  • Rule 6: INDICATION OF NUMBER OF LIGANDS:- In case of chelating ligands like en, which contain the prefixes di, tri, tetra, etc. in their name, the prefixes Bis (for two), Tris (for three), Tetrakis (for four), Pentakis (for five) are used before their name. Ex. [Co(NH₃)₂(en)₂]Cl₃ → diamminebis(ethylenediamine)cobalt(III) chloride, [Co(en)₃]₂(SO₃)₃ → tris(ethylenediamine)cobalt(III) sulphite, [Ni(CO)₄] → tetracarbonylnickel(0), K₄[Fe(CN)₆] → Potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II).
  • Rule 7: NAMING OF AMBIDENTATE LIGANDS:- Named either by special name or by mentioning the donor atom. Ex:- SCN (Thiocyanato-S), NCS (Isothiocyanato-N), NO₂ (Nitro-N), ONO (Nitrito-O).

7. Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

  1. The suitable number of atomic orbitals (s, p, d) of central metal atom/ion hybridise to provide empty hybrid orbitals.
  2. These hybrid orbitals accept lone pair of electrons from the ligands and are directed towards the ligand positions according to the geometry of the complex. Tetrahedral, octahedral, square planar etc.
  3. When inner d-orbitals i.e. (n-1)d orbitals are used in hybridization the complex is called - inner orbital or spin paired complex.
  4. When outer d-orbitals are used in hybridization i.e., nd orbitals the complex is called - outer orbital or high spin / spin free complex.

LIMITATIONS OF VALENCE BOND THEORY
  1. It does not explain the colour exhibited by coordination compounds.
  2. It does not give quantitative interpretation of the thermodynamic or kinetic stabilities of coordination compounds.
  3. It does not distinguish between weak and strong ligands.
  4. It involves a number of assumptions.

8. Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

  1. Crystal field theory explains the metal-ligand bonding in coordination compounds.
  2. It assumes the metal-ligand bonds is electrostatic (ionic in nature) that means attraction between positive charge metal ion and negative charge ligands.
  3. Ligands are treated as point charges (if anionic) and point dipole (if neutral).
  4. Degeneracy: All five d orbitals in an isolated gaseous metal atom/ion have the same energy - they are degenerate.
  5. When ligands come near the metal ion, their negative field interacts with the d-orbitals.
  6. This interaction removes degeneracy - some orbitals get higher energy, others lower. This process is called crystal field splitting.

Crystal Field Splitting in Octahedral Complexes :-

(i) Arrangement:- In an octahedral complex, six ligands surround the central metal ion (like at the corners of an octahedron).

(ii) Repulsion Effect:- Electrons in the ligands repel the electrons in the metal ion's d-orbitals. The repulsion is stronger when a d-orbital points directly towards a ligand. The repulsion is weaker when a d-orbital lies between ligands.

Splitting: The five d-orbitals split into two sets:

eg → (dx²-y², dz²) has higher energy (points directly at ligands).

t2g → (dxy, dyz, dzx) has lower energy (lies between ligands).

The energy difference between these two sets is called crystal field splitting energy (Δo) or (10 Dq).

LIMITATION OF CRYSTAL FIELD THEORY
  1. It assumes metal-ligands bonding is purely ionic and ignores covalent character.
  2. It cannot explain π bonding (donor or acceptor interactions).
  3. It fails to explain the exact color and spectra of all coordination compounds.
  4. It sometimes gives incorrect predictions of magnetic moments.

9. Isomerism in Co-ordination Compound

Compounds having same molecular formula but different structural arrangement in space are known as isomers and phenomena is called isomerism.

1. STRUCTURAL ISOMERISM

1.) Ionization isomerism: Coordination complex which have same empirical formula but give different ions in solution on ionization.

Ex:- [Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄ → [Co(NH₃)₅Br]²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ and [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Br → [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]⁺ + Br⁻

2.) Hydrate isomerism: Similar to ionization isomerism. Arises due to replacement of water molecule from coordination sphere.

Ex:- [Cr(H₂O)₆]Cl₃, [Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]Cl₂·H₂O, [Cr(H₂O)₄Cl₂]Cl·2H₂O

3.) Linkage isomerism: Compounds have same molecular formula but different attachment of ambidentate ligand.

Ex:- [Co(NO₂)(NH₃)₅]Cl₂ and [Co(ONO)(NH₃)₅]Cl₂

4.) Co-ordination isomerism: Observed in compounds comprising both cationic and anionic complex.

Ex:- [Co(NH₃)₆][Cr(CN)₆] and [Cr(NH₃)₆][Co(CN)₆] ; [Cu(NH₃)₄][PtCl₄] and [Pt(NH₃)₄][CuCl₄]

5.) Co-ordination position isomerism: Exhibited by bridged complex from different placement of ligands.

Ex:- [(NH₃)₄Co(μ-OH)₂Co(NH₃)₂Cl₂]SO₄ (unsymmetrical) and [Cl(NH₃)₃Co(μ-OH)₂Co(NH₃)₃Cl]SO₄ (symmetrical).

STEREOISOMERISM [Space Isomerism]

When coordination compound have same molecular formula but they show different arrangement of ligands in space around the central metal ion.

1) Geometrical (Cis/Trans) isomerism: Different spatial arrangement of ligands.

  • Cis Isomer: Two identical ligands are adjacent to each other.
  • Trans Isomer: Two identical ligands are opposite to each other.
